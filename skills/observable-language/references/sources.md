# Sources & lineage

The evidence behind this skill. Read when updating, extending, or challenging it.

The earlier informal research for this topic claimed "containerwoorden" was not a
real term. That is wrong — it's a dictionary headword (below). The concept also
has a much older and deeper lineage than "coaching/NLP jargon": it goes back to
general semantics in 1939.

## The term itself (Dutch)

- **Van Dale / woorden.org** — *containerbegrip*: "begrip zonder scherp
  afgebakende betekenis, waaraan de taalgebruiker zelf nader invulling kan geven
  en dat op veel verschillende toestanden, gebeurtenissen of zaken wordt
  toegepast." Synonyms: *verzamelbegrip*, *paraplubegrip*.
  https://www.woorden.org/woord/containerbegrip
- **WikiWoordenboek** — *containerbegrip* entry.
  https://nl.wiktionary.org/wiki/containerbegrip

*containerwoord* is the informal coaching/feedback variant of the dictionary term
*containerbegrip*.

## Concrete vs abstract — the ladder of abstraction

- **S.I. Hayakawa, *Language in Thought and Action* (1939)** — the ladder of
  abstraction (general semantics, Korzybski lineage). Key nuance this skill
  relies on: no rung is inherently bad; good writing uses the whole range; the
  problem is an abstraction that is *never brought down* to the concrete.
  https://www.toolshero.com/communication-methods/ladder-of-abstraction/
- Mindtools summary of the ladder for communication.
  https://www.mindtools.com/aon6wso/the-ladder-of-abstraction/

## Nominalisations — the NLP Meta Model

- **Meta Model (Bandler & Grinder)**, modelled from Fritz Perls and Virginia
  Satir: a *nominalisation* turns a process (a verb) into an abstract noun,
  "freezing" the activity and deleting the detail. The recovery move is a
  question that turns the noun back into a filmable process — the same "waaraan
  zie je dat?" this skill uses.
  https://nlppod.com/meta-model-deletions-nominalisations/

## Filmable behaviour — the camera / Dead Man's Test

- **The Dead Man's Test** (Applied Behaviour Analysis): "if a dead man can do it,
  it isn't a behaviour." A practical heuristic for whether something is an
  observable behaviour, equivalent to "can you point a camera at it?"
  https://behaviorintervention101.wordpress.com/2013/02/21/what-is-behavior-the-dead-mans-test/
- **Caveat — cite it as a heuristic, not a law.** There is published critique:
  Cuvo, "Requiem for the Dead Man Test?", *Perspectives on Behavior Science*,
  argues the test is imprecise on several counts.
  https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40614-016-0082-5
- Operational definitions (turning broad traits into observable, measurable
  actions) are the constructive counterpart used in the rewrite step.

## The rewrite scaffold — SBI

- **Situation-Behavior-Impact**, Center for Creative Leadership: describe the
  situation (where/when), the *observed* behaviour, and its impact — keeping
  feedback on actions and outcomes rather than personality traits. This is the
  structure the "rewrite" step produces.
  https://www.ccl.org/articles/leading-effectively-articles/sbi-feedback-model-a-quick-win-to-improve-talent-conversations-development/
